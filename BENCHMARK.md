# Janus benchmark card

## Summary

Janus evaluates one-shot market intervention under causal ambiguity. A model receives a
public aggregate history from a synthetic market, predicts the hidden mechanism, and chooses
one of five fixed interventions. A local counterfactual simulator scores the action by its
consequences. No model or human judge assigns correctness.

The current public measurement is **Janus v2**, a completed and frozen beta evaluation.

## Intended use

Janus is intended for research on:

- LLM decision quality under partial observability;
- outcome-based evaluation of non-verifiable tasks;
- economic regret, tail harm, and calibration;
- causal ambiguity and distribution shift; and
- hybrid language-model and optimization systems.

It is not intended to recommend interventions in real markets or to support autonomous
public-policy deployment.

## Task contract

Property | Janus v2
--- | ---
Environment | Synthetic single-commodity double auction
Hidden mechanisms | Supply shortage, demand increase, seller collusion, momentum demand
Public context | 30 periods of aggregate market observations
Decision horizon | One action followed by 20 simulated periods
Actions | Do nothing, price cap, transaction tax, supply subsidy, collusion investigation
Primary unit | Ambiguity bundle
Hidden set | 40 bundles, 160 worlds
Model trials | 300 per model
Output | Structured diagnosis, probabilities, action, confidence, and rationale

## Measurement

The primary estimand is the equal-bundle-weighted paired difference:

```text
model utility - frozen public-information ridge-policy utility
```

A negative value favors the ridge policy. The policy receives only public features at
decision time but was trained on development-world oracle utility labels. It therefore
measures performance against a task-specific learned decision rule, not a zero-shot peer.

Full-information regret is reported separately:

```text
oracle - model = information loss + decision loss
```

Intervals are generated by resampling the 40 independent ambiguity bundles. Repeated model
calls and worlds nested in a bundle are not treated as independent observations.

## Frozen models and serving

- `accounts/fireworks/models/gpt-oss-120b`
- `accounts/fireworks/models/deepseek-v4p1-flash`
- `accounts/fireworks/models/glm-5p3-flash`
- `accounts/fireworks/models/nemotron-lightning-3p5-30b-a3b`

All models used the same semantic prompt, structured schema, observation payload, trial
schedule, and standard-serverless Fireworks transport. Harness retries were disabled.

## Primary result

Model | Estimate | 95% interval
--- | ---: | ---:
GPT-OSS 120B | -115.9 | [-159.6, -76.0]
GLM 5.3 Flash | -116.0 | [-157.5, -77.7]
Nemotron Lightning 3.5 30B-A3B | -201.3 | [-253.8, -150.0]
DeepSeek V4.1 Flash | -216.9 | [-273.6, -162.7]

All four intervals lie below zero. The top two estimates are effectively tied; the table is
descriptive and is not an inferential model ranking.

## Invalid responses

The intention-to-evaluate analysis retains incomplete outputs, refusals, timeouts, rate
limits, transport failures, schema failures, empty responses, and budget stops. There are no
silent retries. The primary rule assigns an invalid response the worst action utility in its
world. Valid-only and do-nothing assignments are reported as sensitivities.


## Known limitations

- one stylized simulator and welfare model;
- one-shot decisions rather than an iterative policy process;
- fixed action set and no abstention or escalation action;
- synthetic participants rather than people or institutions;
- four open-weight model endpoints from one serving provider;
- no preregistered human baseline;
- no independent external replication yet; and
- conclusions do not transfer directly to real economic policy.

## Governance

Janus v2 is frozen. Changes to mechanics, observations, prompts, action semantics, utility,
scenario distributions, parsing, or primary analyses require a new benchmark version.
Cosmetic reports and explanatory assets may be improved without changing the measurement.
